JUDGMENT

1. The appellant was arrested on 4 January 2014 in connection with an FIR registered under Section 420 IPC alleging cheating of investors. His application for bail under Section 439 of the Code of Criminal Procedure was rejected by the High Court. The facts relevant to this appeal are briefly stated.

2. Learned counsel for the appellant contended that the appellant has been in custody for over fourteen months, that the investigation is complete and that the charge sheet has been filed.

3. Learned counsel for the State submitted that the appellant is an influential person and may tamper with the witnesses, many of whom are his former employees.

4. The question that arises for consideration is what factors govern the grant or refusal of bail under Section 439 CrPC.

5. The basic rule is bail, not jail. Personal liberty under Article 21 of the Constitution cannot be curtailed merely because an accusation has been made.

6. While considering an application for bail under Section 439 CrPC, the court must consider the nature and gravity of the accusation, the severity of the punishment in the event of conviction, the existence of a prima facie case, the likelihood of the accused fleeing from justice, and the reasonable apprehension of witnesses being tampered with.

7. Bail may be refused where there is a reasonable apprehension that the accused will tamper with the evidence or influence the witnesses. Such apprehension must rest on material on record and not on mere conjecture.

8. In the present case the State has not placed any material to show that the appellant attempted to contact any witness during the fourteen months of custody.

9. We hold that continued detention of the appellant is not warranted. The appeal is allowed and the appellant is directed to be released on bail on furnishing a bond of Rs. 1,00,000 with two sureties.
