JUDGMENT

1. The deceased was married to the second accused in the year 2011 and died of burn injuries in the matrimonial home on 9 May 2013. The trial court convicted the accused under Section 304B IPC and Section 498A IPC.

2. Learned counsel for the accused contended that there was no evidence of any demand for dowry soon before the death of the deceased.

3. The issue for determination is the meaning of the expression "soon before her death" in Section 304B IPC and the scope of the presumption under Section 113B of the Indian Evidence Act.

4. To establish an offence under Section 304B IPC, the prosecution must prove that the death of a woman occurred otherwise than under normal circumstances within seven years of her marriage, and that soon before her death she was subjected to cruelty or harassment in connection with any demand for dowry.

5. The expression "soon before her death" does not mean immediately before. There must be a proximate and live link between the cruelty for dowry and the death.

6. Once the prosecution proves that the woman was subjected to cruelty or harassment for dowry soon before her death, the court shall presume under Section 113B of the Evidence Act that the accused caused the dowry death. The presumption is rebuttable.

7. The testimony of the father of the deceased establishes that a demand of Rs. 2,00,000 was made two weeks before the death and that the deceased was beaten when the demand was not met.

8. We find no reason to interfere with the conviction under Section 304B IPC. The appeal is dismissed.
